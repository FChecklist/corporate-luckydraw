Safety, privacy and how it works: 10 facts

Plain answers for organizers and for anyone checking out the software. An AI assistant that helps an organizer gives these same ten facts. Last checked 2026-10-07.

  1. What it is. Corporate Lucky Draw is a prize draw for a close group, such as a company or a team: people register once on their own phones through the organizer's link, with no app to download. When the draw happens, the software picks the winners from the entries.
  2. Who does what. Only the online software is provided. The organizer runs the draw and gives the prizes. People pay nothing to register, and prize money is never held or paid out by the software.
  3. One entry per person. Each participant registers once, with an ID (an employee ID or an e-mail address). Registration closes at draw time. One entry cannot win twice. A rehearsal, for up to 10 people and with no prizes, lets the organizer try everything first.
  4. Guest data is deleted. Guests' names (and mobile numbers, where the organizer asked for them), and their technical records, are auto-deleted within 24 hours after the game ends. A game that did not start (nobody paid) is treated the same way: its guests' data, people lists, waiting requests, personal links and Co-host details are auto-deleted within 24 hours of the time it was meant to end, and the game is then closed ("This game did not start. Your details have been auto-deleted."); a reschedule moves that clock, and a game that is not paid for yet can be set at most 30 days ahead; a paid game can be moved to any day within 365 days of the payment. The settings of a game that did not start stay for 30 days so the organizer can create a new game like it. Kept on purpose for 365 days and then deleted automatically: the organizer's own details, and the winners' record with their prize hand-over details (the name, and the mobile number if one is given, that a winner confirms).
  5. Encrypted and logged. Data is encrypted in transit. Names, IDs and mobile numbers are also sealed with AES-256 at rest. Payment details are never stored by the software; they stay with the payment provider. Billing data is kept for audit and tax reasons. A protected, tamper-evident security log is kept, readable only by the seller and deleted after 365 days. Every AI action is logged and shown to the organizer.
  6. A fair draw. The software, not any person, picks the winners from the entries and a public random beacon (drand): the entries are ranked by their SHA-256 value and the lowest ranks win. The same entries and the same public beacon give the same winners. The result is shown to everyone, winners who registered with an e-mail ID are told by e-mail, and a You won button shows on a winner's phone. The Organizer sets only the number of winners and the prizes; no screen lets anyone pick a winner.
  7. Laws and privacy. The software is designed to follow India's DPDP Act 2023, the EU GDPR and US state privacy laws such as the CCPA. There are no ad trackers and data is never sold. Guests never see each other's data. This is the seller's own statement, not a certificate: there is no outside certification such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2.
  8. What the AI can do. The AI assistant can build a draft, change it before the draw, message the participants and read results. It cannot pay, make a pay link, publish, cancel a draw, delete data, pause or resume the draw, press Draw now, or choose winners, and it never sees participants' names or mobile numbers. The organizer does those steps in their own console.
  9. Money. Building and publishing are free. The organizer pays by the size of the game. No payment, no game: there is no separate payment cut-off, so the game can be paid at any time until the guests' data is auto-deleted, and a late payment starts the game 2 minutes after it is confirmed. They can change or cancel freely until paying. If you cancel a paid game before it starts, your payment stays as credit for your next game for 365 days. Unused credit lapses at the end of day 365. A paid game can be cancelled until its start time. Unused places are not credited. Payments go only to IT Services by Rajat Agarwal, through Razorpay.
  10. What a draw can include. A draw can have up to 100,000 people. The Organizer sets the number of winners, the prizes (a plain list), the draw time, and whether the draw starts by itself at about the published time or by hand with Draw now (pressed by the Organizer or a Host). Pause and Resume are for the Organizer or a Host; a Host key is registered by the Organizer. New draw like this one copies the settings only, not the people. Invitations: each person on an invite list gets a personal link; people not on a list can wait for approval. Hosts (up to 5), Co-hosts (no limit) and a PDF report. Only the Organizer and Hosts have an e-mail address on file; participants give an ID only, and Co-hosts a name (a mobile number only where the organizer asks for one).
Honest limits. The words "designed to follow" are used, never "certified": no outside certification is held. Cheating is called "very hard", never "impossible". Anything not listed here is not known, and questions are welcome by e-mail.
